# The Neighbours

**The outcome:** you can say which tool a
given music job belongs to, and stop asking
notation software to be a recording studio
(or vice versa).

## The audio side: Audacity and Ardour

**Audacity** and **Ardour** (both in this
series) work with *sound itself* — recorded
performances, waveforms. The border is
simple once seen: if the thing you are
editing happened in a room (or a synth)
and you can hear it, it's audio. If it is
instructions for a performance that hasn't
happened, it's notation.

The two sides cooperate constantly. A
score's rehearsal MP3 gets trimmed in
Audacity. A transcribed recording becomes a
score here. A DAW takes this guide's MIDI
export and dresses it in far better
instruments than any notation program
carries — a standard route from "written"
to "presentable demo," and the reason MIDI
export (chapter 5) matters more than its
narrow format suggests.

## LilyPond: notation as text

**LilyPond** is the other free notation
engine, and the philosophical opposite:
you write code-like text (`c'4 e' g'`) and
it engraves beautiful pages. No window, no
mouse, superb typography, a learning curve
like a cliff face, and total scriptability
— the converter in chapter 9 is MuseScore
gesturing at what LilyPond is made of.
Writers who live in text editors and
version control often end up there;
writers who think in pages end up here.
MusicXML and MIDI pass between them.

## The commercial notation programs

**Dorico**, **Sibelius** and the remaining
professional tools (Finale, the category's
elder, was discontinued in 2024 and its
users were steered to Dorico) sell polish
the free program doesn't promise:
house-style engraving controls for
publishers, film-scoring workflows,
support contracts, and decades of
professional habit. Working professionals
choose them for those reasons and are not
wrong. MuseScore's counter-offer is that
it is free, opens their exports via
MusicXML, and — for the school, church,
community and home work that is most of
the world's notation — is simply enough.
If you outgrow it, your `.mscz` files'
contents travel out through MusicXML
(chapter 5); nothing here is a trap.

## Guitar tablature tools

Guitarists meet notation through **tab**,
and dedicated tab editors (Guitar Pro, and
free ones such as TuxGuitar) are many
players' first writing tools. MuseScore
imports Guitar Pro files and can show a
tab staff alongside notation, which makes
it a fine second home for tab-born music —
and the exchange exposes the same lesson
as MIDI: tab says where fingers go, not
what the music is, and the conversion is a
reading, not a copy. (This series' shelf
may one day hold a tab guide of its own;
until then, this paragraph is the whole
signpost.)

## And the paper

The oldest neighbour still wins some
arguments: a printed part doesn't crash,
need charging, or update its terms of
service. Chapter 8's PDF exists to become
paper. The program's job is to make the
paper right.
