# Glossary

**AppImage** — a Linux packaging format: the
whole program in one file, run directly. This
guide's lab extracted one with
`--appimage-extract` because its sandbox
could not mount it the usual way.

**Concert pitch** — music as it sounds, not
as a transposing instrument's part is
written. The score view's Concert pitch
switch chooses between the two; parts are
always at written pitch.

**Converter** — MuseScore's command-line
mode: open a score, write it out as another
format, no window. Chapter 9.

**Excerpt** — MuseScore's internal name for
a generated part: one player's view of the
score, stored inside the same file. A score
with no excerpts has no parts yet.

**Measure / bar** — the box of music between
two barlines; the unit rehearsals count in.

**Mixer** — the panel holding each staff's
playback sound, volume and pan. Playback
settings, not notation.

**MS Basic** — the bundled soundfont that
ships with every install; this lab verified
it is the only soundfont inside the AppImage.

**`.mscz` / `.mscx`** — MuseScore's native
formats: the score. `.mscz` is a zip archive
containing the `.mscx` (the music as XML),
style, thumbnail and settings.

**MuseHub** — the companion app that
delivers MuseSounds and other add-ons.
Optional for notation itself.

**MuseSounds** — the larger, separately
downloaded sample libraries for playback.

**MusicXML** — the standard interchange
format for notation (`.musicxml`, `.mxl`):
the way scores travel between programs.

**Palette** — the panel of insertable
elements (clefs, dynamics, repeats, text…)
on the window's left side.

**Part** — two meanings, kept apart in this
guide by context: an instrument's line
within the score, and the generated sheet
that one player reads.

**Score** — the document: all the parts, as
one body of music, plus its layout and
playback settings.

**Soundfont** — a file of sampled instrument
sounds (`.sf2`, `.sf3`) that a player uses to
turn notes into audio.

**Staff / staves** — the five lines one
instrument's music is written on.

**Transpose** — to move music to a different
key or interval, notes, spelling and key
signatures together.

**Voice** — an independent rhythmic line
within one staff; a staff can hold up to
four, for layered music on one line.
